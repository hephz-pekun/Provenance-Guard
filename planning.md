# Provenance Guard - Planning Document

# Project Overview

Provenance Guard is a content attribution system designed to estimate whether a submitted piece of writing was likely written by a human or generated by AI.

Rather than making definitive claims about authorship, the system combines multiple independent detection signals into a confidence score and communicates uncertainty through transparent user-facing labels.

The primary goals of the system are:

- Transparency
- Confidence reporting
- Appeals and contestability
- Auditability
- Rate limiting and abuse prevention
- Responsible AI attribution

The goal is not perfect AI detection. Instead, the system provides an explainable attribution workflow that allows creators to understand and challenge system decisions.

---

# Architecture Narrative

When a creator submits content through the `POST /submit` endpoint, the text is passed through two independent detection signals.

The first signal is an LLM-based classifier powered by GPT-OSS-20B through Groq. This evaluates semantic characteristics such as coherence, consistency, and common AI writing patterns.

The second signal is a stylometric analysis module that evaluates structural properties of the text, including sentence-length variance and vocabulary diversity.

The outputs of both signals are combined into a single confidence score.

The confidence score is then mapped to one of three attribution categories:

- Likely Human
- Uncertain
- Likely AI

The classification result, confidence score, and component scores are recorded in an audit log and returned to the user.

If a creator disagrees with a classification, they can submit an appeal through the `POST /appeal` endpoint. The appeal updates the content status to `under_review` and stores the creator's reasoning in the audit log.

# Detection Signals

## Signal 1: LLM-Based Classification (GPT-OSS-20B via Groq)

### What It Measures

The language-model signal evaluates:

- Writing coherence
- Semantic consistency
- Predictability
- Common AI writing patterns
- Generic phrasing

### Output

```text
0.0 = Strongly Human
1.0 = Strongly AI
```

Example:

```json
{
  "llm_score": 0.82
}
```

### Why It Was Chosen

Large language models can identify subtle semantic and stylistic patterns that are difficult to capture using manually defined rules.

### Blind Spots

- Highly polished human writing may appear AI-generated.
- Heavily edited AI-written content may appear human-written.
- Results are probabilistic rather than definitive proof.

---

## Signal 2: Stylometric Analysis

### What It Measures

The stylometric signal evaluates measurable writing characteristics.

#### Sentence-Length Variance

Human writing often varies substantially in sentence length.

#### Type-Token Ratio (Vocabulary Diversity)

Measures vocabulary diversity by comparing unique words to total words.

#### Overall Structural Consistency

Evaluates whether the writing follows highly regular or highly varied patterns.

### Output

```text
0.0 = Strongly Human
1.0 = Strongly AI
```

Example:

```json
{
  "stylometric_score": 0.64
}
```

### Why It Was Chosen

This signal is independent from the language model and provides a measurable statistical perspective.

### Blind Spots

- Poetry may appear AI-generated.
- Academic writing can appear unusually structured.
- Very short texts provide insufficient evidence.

---

# Confidence Scoring

## Combined Confidence Formula

The final confidence score is calculated using weighted averaging:

```text
confidence =
(0.6 × llm_score)
+
(0.4 × stylometric_score)
```

### Rationale

The language-model signal receives a larger weight because it captures broader semantic patterns.

The stylometric signal serves as an independent structural cross-check and prevents complete dependence on a single model.

---

# Uncertainty Representation

## Confidence Score Meaning

The confidence score reflects how closely content resembles AI-generated writing.

The score is not proof of authorship.

---

## Attribution Thresholds

| Confidence Score | Attribution |
|------------------|-------------|
| 0.00 - 0.39 | Likely Human |
| 0.40 - 0.69 | Uncertain |
| 0.70 - 1.00 | Likely AI |

---

## False Positive Mitigation

Incorrectly labeling human content as AI-generated is considered more harmful than failing to identify AI-generated content.

To reduce false positives:

- A large uncertainty region is used.
- Borderline scores remain uncertain.
- Appeals are available for all classifications.
- Results are presented as estimates rather than definitive judgments.

# Anticipated Edge Cases

## Edge Case 1: Poetry

Poetry often contains:

- Repetition
- Limited vocabulary
- Non-standard structure

These characteristics may resemble AI-generated patterns.

---

## Edge Case 2: Academic Writing

Formal academic writing frequently:

- Uses predictable structure
- Follows strict conventions
- Contains specialized vocabulary

The system may assign elevated AI-likelihood scores.

---

## Edge Case 3: Heavily Edited AI Content

AI-generated text may be significantly revised by a human author.

The result may contain both human-like and AI-like characteristics, producing uncertain classifications.

---

## Edge Case 4: Very Short Submissions

Short submissions provide insufficient evidence for:

- Vocabulary analysis
- Sentence-length analysis
- Reliable model evaluation

These should generally trend toward uncertain classifications.

---

# Rate Limiting Plan

## Proposed Limits

```text
10 requests per minute
100 requests per day
```

### Reasoning

A typical creator submits only a small number of drafts during a writing session.

These limits allow normal usage while helping prevent:

- Automated abuse
- Mass submissions
- Resource exhaustion attacks

# Stretch Feature: Ensemble Detection

## Goal

Improve attribution quality by incorporating a third independent detection signal into the confidence-scoring pipeline.

The system currently uses:

1. LLM-Based Classification
2. Stylometric Analysis

The stretch feature adds:

3. Repetition Analysis

This creates an ensemble detection pipeline that combines multiple independent signals.

---

## Signal 3: Repetition Analysis

### What It Measures

The repetition signal measures how frequently words are repeated within a piece of writing.

AI-generated content often uses repeated words, phrases, and sentence structures more consistently than human-written content.

### Output

```text
0.0 = Strongly Human
1.0 = Strongly AI
```

Example:

```json
{
  "repetition_score": 0.42
}
```

### Why It Was Chosen

This signal is:

- Independent from the LLM classifier
- Independent from stylometric analysis
- Easy to compute
- Interpretable

### Blind Spots

- Poetry may contain intentional repetition.
- Persuasive writing often repeats key ideas.
- Very short text provides little evidence.

---

## Ensemble Weighting

The final confidence score will be calculated using:

```text
confidence =
(0.5 × llm_score)
+
(0.3 × stylometric_score)
+
(0.2 × repetition_score)
```

### Rationale

The LLM remains the strongest signal because it evaluates semantic patterns.

The stylometric signal provides structural analysis.

The repetition signal serves as an additional behavioral indicator.

---

## Validation Plan

The ensemble detector will be tested using:

1. AI-generated text
2. Human-written text
3. Academic writing
4. Conversational writing

Verification will confirm:

- All three signals generate values.
- Confidence scores vary across writing styles.
- The new signal influences final decisions.
